Reading Notes: The Anemoia Device: A Tangible AI System for the Co-creation of Synthetic Memories through Scent
Cyrus Clarke, Jianing Yu, Melo Chen, Yuen Zou & Hiroshi Ishii (MIT / Harvard), NeurIPS 2025 Creative AI Track.

1 · Why I Read This Paper
This paper presents an AI-driven interactive installation that takes photographs as input and produces non-visual sensory experiences as output. Unlike conventional generative AI artworks that seek to produce new visual images, it translates the semantic content of photographs into scents, giving material form to sensory experiences that remain invisible within photographic images.

For me, it constitutes both a case study concerning the locus of photographic existence and an example of how technological pipelines, perceptual experiences, and artistic concepts can be integrated into a practice-based research paper.
2 · Summary
- What the paper is about: The Anemoia Device translates archival photographs into narratives and scents, attempting to evoke nostalgia for past experiences that the participant has never personally lived through.
- Main contributions: The authors establish a multimodal system integrating visual understanding, language generation, scent matching, and physical output. Tangible rotary controls provide participants with a degree of creative agency.
- What problem it addresses and why it matters: Interactions with generative AI are predominantly mediated through screens and textual input. The authors attempt to establish an alternative form of interaction characterised by materiality, bodily engagement, and ritual.
- Innovation and benefits: The photograph no longer functions as a final visual object but becomes a medium through which another form of sensory experience is generated.
- What it advances: The project extends cross-modal generation beyond transformations between digital images and texts into physical olfactory experiences.
3 · Critical Thinking
- The problematic definition of synthetic memory. Can nostalgia for an event that one has never personally experienced genuinely be called a memory? Or is it better understood as an imagined experience constructed through images, narratives, and scents?
- The subjectivity of cross-modal translation. A photograph of a coastline might evoke seawater, vegetation, humid air, or a personally familiar scent. There is no necessary correspondence between an image and a particular smell. The system therefore produces a new sensory interpretation rather than reconstructing the original olfactory experience associated with the photograph.
- Cultural biases embedded in the model. The AI's interpretation of photographic scenes and its associations with scents may rely on culturally specific semantic relationships. People from different geographical and cultural backgrounds may associate entirely different smells with concepts such as old houses, rainy days, or childhood.
- The boundaries of participant agency. Tangible rotary controls provide a more embodied interaction than text-based interfaces. Nevertheless, the parameters associated with these controls and the available generative space remain predetermined by the designers.
- The distinction between artistic intention and experiential evidence. The installation's ability to generate scents does not establish that participants necessarily experience synthetic memories or nostalgia. Such claims require additional experiential research.
4 · Creative Thinking
- New ideas inspired by the paper: Photography can function as a cross-sensory medium. Although a photograph records optical information, viewing it may evoke memories of temperature, sound, smell, and even bodily position.
- Methodological transfer: In my photographic system, non-visual information contained in written memories could be translated into visual forms. For example, coldness need not be represented exclusively through blue tones; it might instead be expressed through low contrast, perceived spatial distance, or the partial disappearance of an image.
- Alternative approaches to the same problem: The Anemoia Device translates photographs into new sensory outputs. My research could reverse this direction by translating users' non-visual memories back into the imaging parameters of a photograph.
- New research directions: The locus of photographic existence could be extended from the image plane to relationships between different senses. A photographic artwork might therefore possess no singular final visual form but instead constitute a collection of perceptual potentials that can be repeatedly activated.
- Implications for installation design: Rather than relying exclusively on a web-based text interface, I could develop my system into a physical photographic apparatus incorporating rotary controls, optical components, displays, or printing mechanisms. This could introduce more explicit bodily participation and ritual into the process of image generation.
5 · Form (Reading the Paper as a Genre)
- Structural characteristics: The paper centres on its artistic concept, tangible system, interaction design, and multimodal technical implementation.
- First appearance of the contribution claim: The abstract introduces the installation and its creative objectives, followed by an explanation of the technical pipeline and the significance of embodied interaction.
- Artwork description vs. analysis: Installation design and technical implementation constitute the principal research contributions, while theoretical questions provide the conceptual foundation for the system.
- Approach to related work: An interdisciplinary context is established through discussions of memory, olfactory experience, tangible interaction, and generative technologies.
- How the process is evidenced: The existence and technical feasibility of the artwork are demonstrated through the physical installation, interaction procedures, system outputs, and technical pipeline.
- Figures: Fig. 1 presents the overall installation on the left and six stages of user interaction on the right, including photo input, semantic generation, rotary control, narrative and scent generation, scent output, and smelling. This visual arrangement simultaneously communicates the appearance of the artwork, its methodology, and its experiential process.
- Person and tense: The first-person plural is employed to introduce the system and articulate its creative contributions.
- Limitations: It is necessary to distinguish the technical feasibility of the system from whether psychological experiences such as nostalgia and synthetic remembering actually occur.
6 · Venue Signal
The NeurIPS Creative AI Track provides a publication context for the intersection of generative technologies and artistic practice that differs from conventional machine learning research.

This paper demonstrates that a creative research contribution does not necessarily require the development of a new foundation model. Instead, it can emerge from the combination of existing models, tangible installations, cross-modal translation, and distinctive forms of interaction.

However, this individual case does not establish that the venue generally considers evaluation unnecessary.

For my own system, the significance of AI may lie less in its generative capabilities than in its ability to reorganise photographic practices and users' experiences of photographs.
7 · Take-home
- What I learned: Existing AI models can generate independent creative research contributions through novel combinations of media.
- What was new to me: Translating photographic images into olfactory experiences instead of producing another photograph.
- What I can apply to my research: Establishing cross-modal mappings between subjective memory semantics and medium-specific photographic properties, while strengthening bodily participation in the image-processing experience.
- Whether the structure is worth emulating: Yes. It is particularly useful as a reference for practice-based research intended for Creative AI, SIGGRAPH Art Papers, or other venues specialising in artistic and interactive systems.
