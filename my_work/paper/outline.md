A photograph begins as darkness. A latent image on film appears only through chemical development; the electrical signal from a digital sensor becomes a picture only after a chain of algorithms. We tend to see this process as truth gradually emerging, and imaging engineers calibrate each step against colour charts, standard observers and error metrics until it is "correct". Yet every step is a choice that keeps one possibility and deletes others. Noise reduction deletes noise. Vignette correction deletes the darkness at the edge of the lens. White balance deletes the colour of the light. Demosaicing estimates two of every pixel's three colour values, because the sensor measured only one. Memory is a second black box. It enlarges what mattered, drops what did not, and reorganises itself with each retelling, so it rarely agrees with the photograph. This work treats that disagreement as a lead. Where memory departs from the record, it often points to a choice inside the camera. Following it back through the pipeline exposes what the machine chose and what it deleted. Neither the photograph nor the memory is the truth. They are two developments of one moment, in two black boxes.

# Making Photographs Feel Like Memories: Image Reworking Through Personal Narratives

## 1. Introduction and Research Question

Photographs record scenes through technical processes such as exposure, white balance, focusing, and computational processing. Human memory, however, is influenced by emotion, attention, and the passage of time. As a result, what a photograph depicts often differs from how an individual remembers the experience.

**Research Question:**

How can personal narratives be translated into image-processing operations, and how can photographic re-development and generative techniques make the differences between photographic records and subjective memories visible?

## 2. Theoretical Background and Related Work

This research draws on three main areas:

- **Photography and Technology:** How cameras determine the final image through parameter selection and computational processing.
- **Photography and Memory:** How personal memory selects, reconstructs, and sometimes transforms past visual experiences.
- **Generative Imaging:** How AI can supplement information absent from photographic records, and how such additions affect photographic authenticity and our understanding of memory.

By examining relevant photographic theories, memory studies, and artistic practices, the research establishes its methodological position.

## 3. Methodology

This research develops a creative process that translates personal recollections into photographic modifications, using photographs and narratives provided by participants.

### Step 1: Collecting Photographs and Memories

A small number of participants will be invited to provide personal photographs. They will first describe their memories without looking at the photographs, then compare their recollections with the images to identify differences. Key statements will be documented.

### Step 2: Translating Memories into Imaging Operations

Based on these narratives, the photographs will be processed through three types of operations:

1. **Re-select:** Adjusting parameters such as exposure, white balance, and colour to bring existing visual information closer to the recollection.
2. **Undo:** Reprocessing RAW data to reverse selected computational corrections made by the camera and explore alternative visual representations.
3. **Exceed:** Using generative AI to supplement information that cannot be recovered from the original photographic data, while explicitly marking the generated regions.

### Step 3: Feedback and Iteration

Participants will review the modified photographs and discuss which elements feel closer to their memories and which introduce new discrepancies. The process and different versions of the images will be documented.

## 4. Results and Analysis

Each case study will present the original photograph, key narrative statements, the modified image, and documentation of the corresponding imaging operations and generated regions.

By comparing the original photograph, direct AI editing, and the proposed method, the analysis will examine:

- How personal narratives influence the visual representation of photographs.
- How different imaging operations reshape the relationship between photographs and memories.
- How each modification and its narrative source can remain visible and traceable.

## 5. Discussion and Limitations

The discussion will address the challenges of translating subjective experiences into technical parameters, as well as the interpretive role of the artist in this process.

It will also consider the technical limitations of RAW data, the authenticity of AI-generated content, the potential influence of modified photographs on subsequent recollections, and the limitations of working with a small number of participants.

## 6. Conclusion and Contribution

This research proposes a **narrative-driven method of photographic reworking that combines RAW re-development with generative image processing**.

Its contribution is not to reconstruct an objectively accurate memory, but to make visible the process through which personal recollections reshape photographic images.

By distinguishing between the original photographic record, parameter-based modifications, and generated content, the method provides a way to examine and discuss the relationship between photographic recording and subjective memory.
