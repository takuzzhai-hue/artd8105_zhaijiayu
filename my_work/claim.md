### Version 1 — Four-sentence claim

**I made…** I made a five-slide proposal for a photographic system in which three predefined recollection narratives are translated into imaging parameters, applied as independent branches to one source photograph, and brought together through spatial coexistence rules.

**In making it, I found…** In specifying the workflow, I found that generating multiple versions is not the same design problem as allowing them to coexist: the proposal needs an explicit compositing rule in addition to narrative-to-parameter mapping and independent branching.

**This means…** This suggests that coexistence should be treated as a distinct, testable operation in the proposed computational-imaging method, rather than assumed to follow automatically from producing several versions.

**This is useful for…** This is useful for structuring my next practice-based photographic experiments and for documenting which visual effects come from narrative mapping, branch generation, or the rule used to combine branches.

### Agent challenge — evidence audit

**Challenge 1: Does the five-slide proposal prove that multiple photographic states can coexist?**  
No. It names a compositing stage, but contains no implemented system or image comparisons. It can support a claim about the *need to specify* a coexistence operation, not about successful coexistence.

**Challenge 2: Does a predefined narrative reliably imply particular focus, light, and detail parameters?**  
Not yet. The mapping is a design choice. It must be made explicit, logged, and tested for reproducibility; it should not be presented as a universal model of human remembering.

**Challenge 3: Does the project meet the brief's 'earlier class project with an output or prototype' requirement?**  
Only partially. The five-slide proposal is an inspectable class-project output, but not an imaging prototype. If the instructor expects an implemented earlier project, this selection needs instructor approval or replacement with a finished prototype.

**Challenge 4: What is actually defensible now?**  
The current artifact shows that the proposed method distinguishes between the creation of different branches and their spatial combination. It does not establish the visual or experiential value of that distinction.

**Challenge 5 — sentence 3, checked against the same slides on 21 October 2026:**  
Version 2 says coexistence "cannot be inferred from the existence of multiple branches alone." The slides show that this proposal writes C3 as its own stage. They do not show that inference is impossible. A skeptic can say the C2/C3 split is only a heading until the same branches are combined under two rules and the images differ. Version 3 narrows that one clause to what the proposal itself does.

### Version 2 — Revised four-sentence claim

**I made…** I made a five-slide design proposal for a rule-based photographic workflow that maps three predefined recollection narratives to imaging parameters, generates separate states from one source photograph, and proposes combining those states within a single image.

**In making it, I found…** In organising this workflow, I identified a design distinction between generating different photographic states and defining how those states share the same image space.

**This means…** For this proposed method, spatial coexistence must be specified and evaluated as its own rule-based stage; it cannot be inferred from the existence of multiple branches alone.

**This is useful for…** This distinction provides a concrete structure for my next practice-based experiment: comparing three coexistence rules while keeping the source photograph and recollection scenarios constant.
