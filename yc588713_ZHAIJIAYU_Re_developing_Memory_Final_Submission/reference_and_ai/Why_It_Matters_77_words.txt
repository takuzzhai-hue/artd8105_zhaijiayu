Photographs usually appear as finished outputs, concealing the computational choices through which they become visible. Re-developing Memory turns those choices into artistic material and connects them to personal recollection. By distinguishing RAW-derived renderings, non-generative photographic interventions, and AI-generated additions, the work makes image provenance part of its visual language. Its staged, strip-based composition invites the SIGGRAPH community to reconsider photographic finality, the expressive potential of imaging pipelines, and the ethical boundary between recorded evidence and computational imagination.
