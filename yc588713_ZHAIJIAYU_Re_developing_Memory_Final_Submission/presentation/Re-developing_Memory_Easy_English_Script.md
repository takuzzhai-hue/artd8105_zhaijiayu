# Re-developing Memory — Easy English Presentation Script

## Slide 01 — Re-developing Memory (0:25)

Hello. My project is called Re-developing Memory. It starts with one photo and a written memory. The system changes the photo step by step. I ask a simple question: Can one photo have more than one final form?

## Slide 02 — Research question (0:40)

We usually see a photo as a finished image. But a digital camera makes many choices about light, color, and detail. We only see one result. Memory is different. We may remember movement, blur, or something outside the frame. I want to let memory take part in making the photo again.

## Slide 03 — Artistic context (0:45)

My research connects three ideas. First, a camera has its own image process. Second, memory is not always clear or complete. Third, artists can use blur, empty space, and image errors as a visual language. My work starts with an existing photo. It does not make a whole new picture from words alone.

## Slide 04 — System process (0:50)

Here is my system. First, a person uploads a photo and writes a memory. AI reads the photo and the words. It chooses an image effect and the area to change. Then the system changes the image step by step. Every step is saved. At the end, it joins vertical strips from different steps into one final photograph.

## Slide 05 — Three image sources (0:55)

I divide the effects into three groups. R means RAW: we develop real camera data in different ways. We need an original RAW file for this. E means editing. We change the existing photo with blur, white areas, or cuts. G means generation. AI adds something that was not in the photo. This part is a guess, not a fact. Keeping these groups separate is important.

## Slide 06 — Pendulum — time (0:50)

This photo shows a pendulum. The memory says the room was darker, the light was softer, and the ball was moving. The system changes the light and then adds motion blur and a vignette. The camera freezes one moment. But the memory includes the feeling of time. The new image expresses this feeling. It does not measure the real movement.

## Slide 07 — Hand and finger — absence and touch (1:00)

These two examples are different. First, the memory says: I want her hand to disappear. The system covers the hand with white. It does not repair the background. So the missing hand still has a shape. In the second example, the original photo has no finger. But the memory describes touching the buildings with one finger. AI adds that finger. It is not proof of the past. It is a visual form of the story.

## Slide 08 — Forest — uncertain space (0:50)

In the forest example, the person cannot clearly remember the space. The text asks for a changed view and broken parts. The system extends the scene and moves image fragments. Some lines do not match. I keep these visible breaks. The work does not pretend to show the real place again. It shows how uncertain memory can look.

## Slide 09 — Findings and limitations (0:45)

My project is not a tool to find the true past. It is a way to make a photographic poem. The meaning comes from choices: what stays, what disappears, and what is added. AI can make mistakes, and new image parts are not facts. I want to show these changes clearly. My goal is to make the process of remembering visible. Thank you.

## Short Q&A

**What is new about this work?**

The new part is the artistic method. Memory controls a series of image changes. We keep the steps visible in one photographic work.

**Does the system recover real memories?**

No. It makes a visual interpretation of a written memory. It does not prove what happened.

**Are all the effects from a real RAW camera pipeline?**

No. RAW changes need the original sensor data. Other effects are photo edits or AI additions. I keep them separate.

**Where is AI used?**

AI helps read the photo and text, select image operations, find regions, and add requested content when needed. The other effects use normal image processing.

**What are the limitations?**

AI may choose the wrong object or add an unwanted detail. Generated content is not historical evidence. The visual result is an artistic interpretation.
